IV. Estoppel: The Disappearing Commitments

The doctrine of estoppel prevents a party from making a promise, letting others rely on it, and then taking it back when it becomes inconvenient. In his original filing the Respondent made specific written commitments to the full league (Fact 3). The League Office relied on them and entered them as a Consent Agreement in Notice SHADY-2026-001.02. The Respondent now says "Disregard my previous statement" and "I do not agree to the compliance demands set forth in your letter" (Facts 4 and 6).

The Respondent is estopped. A manager cannot sign a deal in the group chat and then void it by firing the guy who typed it. If that worked, every trade in this league would come with a lawyer. The Consent Agreement stands.

V. Still No Precedent

The Respondent's amended filing still claims the league "previously tolerated, and in some cases openly celebrated, tanking strategies that produced similarly depressed Potential Points without formal sanction." It still names no example.

The League Office already did that research for him. Notice SHADY-2026-001.02 identified 47 regular-season starts from 2021 to 2025 (Weeks 1–14) where the player never took the field: 43 inactive, injured or not on an NFL team, and 4 on a bye. Only 2 were the Respondent's. The leading authorities are as follows:

  • In re joebags85, 2023 Shady. Rptr. Wk. 14 (SHADYNASTY 2023). Respondent started C.J. Beathard and Aaron Jones. Neither took the field. No notice issued. Held: a manager who forgot two guys in Week 14 was bad at this, which is not a violation.
  • In re DetroitDumpsterFires, 2025 Shady. Rptr. Wk. 14 (SHADYNASTY 2025). Manager started Jaxson Dart and Devin Singletary during the Giants' bye week. Held: not reading the schedule is a personal failing, not a scheme.
  • In re ElJefe1985, 2024 Shady. Rptr. Wk. 12 (SHADYNASTY 2024). Manager started Joe Burrow on Cincinnati's bye. Held: see above.

Each of those is one bad week by one distracted manager. None involved five unavailable players added in one day, followed by two written filings defending it. The Respondent's precedents are not precedents. They are alibis for other people.

VII. Ruling on Potential Points (Canons of Construction)

The Respondent argues: "The Bylaws establish Potential Points as the intentional mechanism for assessing team quality for draft order." He adds: "Managing a roster in a manner that results in lower Potential Points is not “intentional sabotage”; it is a foreseeable and previously accepted outcome of the system the league chose." The relevant text:

§ Tanking: "Draft order for non-playoff teams is determined by inverse of end of season optimal/potential points to assess true value/quality of teams."
§ Tanking: "Potential points are based on if teams had started their optimal lineup every week."

The League Office applies three canons of construction.

A. Plain meaning. The rule says what Potential Points are for: "to assess true value/quality of teams." True value. Not manufactured value, and not value you hid on IR to bring back in 2028.

B. Purpose. The league ranks non-playoff teams by what they could have scored instead of their record so that an unlucky team, one that lost close games or ran into somebody's career week, is not punished in the draft. The rule exists to protect bad luck. It does not reward engineering. The Bylaws state their own purpose, which is to "ensure a fair and fun league for all owners." A plan to hand yourself the 1.01 by building a roster that cannot score is many things. Fair to all owners is not one of them.

C. No absurd results. A rule should not be read in a way that produces an absurd result. Under the Respondent's reading, the best possible strategy for a non-playoff team is to roster nothing but injured players and start them every week, and that would be "a foreseeable and previously accepted outcome of the system." A rule designed to measure true team quality cannot also be a rule that rewards fielding no team. The Respondent's reading is rejected.
